Multiple Choice Questions

1. A community development officer in Tanzania is advising a group of rural farmers on how to view their farming activities. She emphasizes that farming is not just a traditional way of life, but a commercial enterprise aimed at maximizing net returns through the efficient management of land, labour, water, and capital. Which core aspect of agriculture is the officer emphasizing?
  • A) Agriculture as a pure science
  • B) Agriculture as a traditional art
  • C) Agriculture as a business
  • D) Agriculture as a biological study
  • E) Agriculture as a hobby
Answer: C) Agriculture as a business
Explanation:
Modern agriculture views farming not merely as a subsistence activity or traditional way of life, but as an economic enterprise designed to generate profit through optimized resource allocation.
2. 3. A farmer in Tanzania wants to establish a maize field measuring 10,000 m² using a standard spacing of 90 cm between rows and 30 cm between plants within a row. Following the recommended formula, what will be the correct plant population per hectare for this field?
  • A) 1,111 plants/ha
  • B) 27,000 plants/ha
  • C) 37,037 plants/ha
  • D) 45,500 plants/ha
  • E) 10,000 plants/ha
Answer: C) 37,037 plants/ha
Explanation:
Plant population is calculated by dividing the area of one hectare ($10,000 m^2$) by the product of the inter-row and intra-row spacing ($0.9 m \times 0.3 m = 0.27 m^2$), yielding approximately 37,037 plants.

14. An agronomist is examining the mode of pollination for a new crop variety where pollen transfer occurs from one flower to the stigma of another flower on a separate plant, as seen in maize and avocados. How is this mode of pollination classified?

  • C) Asexual propagation
  • D) Vegetative reproduction

Answer: B) Cross-pollination
Explanation:
Cross-pollination involves the transfer of pollen grains from the anther of one flower to the stigma of a flower on a completely different plant of the same species.

21. A commercial farmer decides to practice mono-cropping (continuous cropping) by growing only one type of crop on a specific piece of land over a long period. What is a major disadvantage of this system as highlighted in the text?
  • A) It makes farm mechanization extremely difficult.
  • B) It requires no labor during harvesting.
  • C) If the crop fails due to weather or pests, the farmer is likely to get a poor harvest.
  • D) It automatically increases soil nitrogen without fertilizers.
  • E) It completely eliminates the need for weed control.
Answer: C) If the crop fails due to weather or pests, the farmer is likely to get a poor harvest.
Explanation:
Relying on a single crop leaves the entire farming enterprise vulnerable to total failure if specific pests, diseases, or adverse weather conditions strike.
22. A smallholder farmer practices mixed cropping by growing two or more crops simultaneously in the same field without any distinct row arrangement. What is one of the primary advantages of this practice?
  • A) It makes the application of specific fertilizers very easy.
  • B) It reduces the risk of crop failure due to environmental stress.
  • C) It completely automates the harvesting process.
  • D) It allows precise tractor spraying for individual crops.
  • E) It eliminates all competition among resources.
Answer: B) It reduces the risk of crop failure due to environmental stress.
Explanation:
Mixed cropping diversifies production on the same land area, ensuring that if one crop suffers, others may survive and yield output.
23. An agricultural extension worker advises a farmer to adopt inter-cropping, where a main crop and an inter-crop are grown simultaneously with distinct row arrangements. According to the principles of inter-cropping, what should the inter-crop preferably be?

Combining cereals with legumes in inter-cropping optimizes space while allowing nitrogen fixation to sustain and enrich soil nutrient levels.
